ReNewGenie
Final-year project · B.Tech CSE (AI/ML) · AKGEC Ghaziabad

Turn waste into value.
Snap it, sort it, sell it.

ReNewGenie is a web app that looks at a photo of waste and tells you what it is, how to recycle it, what scrap dealers would pay and where to drop it off in Delhi NCR. It also shows how recycling could earn carbon credits. Ask it anything in plain words: an on-device AI Copilot understands the request and runs the right tool.

Market intelligence: what the public price lists say

Two public Delhi lists for the same materials. Both are online price lists, not audited quotes. List A is dated 6 October 2026 and says Delhi rates closely follow MCX commodity prices and sit about 3 to 5% above the national average. List B has no date.

  • Metals vary most between the lists: B asks 25% to 50% more for aluminium, brass and copper yet 23% less for iron. That spread is why the demo compares several collectors before you book.
  • Paper and plastic agree within about 7%, and e-waste within about 13%, so those quotes are the most dependable here.
  • Prices follow metal markets and moisture, dirt and mixing. Treat any list as a starting point and ask for a weighing slip.

Price forecast

Scrap rates move with world metal prices, so this lab forecasts them with three methods, tests each on the past, and shows how uncertain the future really is.

What this is, and is not. There is no public history of Delhi scrap rates, so the lab runs on real monthly world prices for six metals (World Bank commodity data, 1980 to June 2017, USD per tonne, via the open datasets/commodity-prices repository). The data ends in 2017, so it shows the method, not today's market. Paste your own monthly rates (at least 48) and the same tests run on them.
Method. Naive repeats the last price. Damped Holt fits a level and a fading trend to log prices. The ridge model regresses the next h-month return on the last six monthly returns. The shaded range is the 10th to 90th percentile of each method's own backtest errors at that horizon, so it reflects real past misses, not a textbook formula. I did not add a neural network: with one price per month and about 450 points it would overfit.

Problem and objective

Waste generation is projected to rise 70% by 2050, yet recycling rates in developing countries often stay below 20%. Existing tools either classify waste or give disposal advice or locate centres, but not all three. ReNewGenie combines image-based classification, education, a collector marketplace and a centre locator in one web platform.

Gap 1Classification models work in isolation, with no user guidance.
Gap 2Disposal advice portals lack an intelligent image front end.
Gap 3No single platform joins classification, education, location services and a market.

CNN learning curve

Training accuracy reaches about 97% while validation accuracy stays near 80%: the network has begun to memorise the 1,770 training photos, which is why augmentation and the ensemble help.

  1. The split is by photo: 70% train, 15% validation, 15% test, random seed 7. A photo and its mirror always stay on the same side.
  2. The CNN checkpoint with the best validation accuracy was kept. The test set was used once, at the end.
  3. Training ran on a 2-core CPU in about 20 minutes. No pretrained weights were available, so there is no transfer learning here, unlike the VGG16 approach in the original report (see the Colab notebook in the repo).
  4. Both models run in your browser in plain JavaScript. I checked that it gives the same probabilities as the Python code.

Limits: one split, and only 16 Trash photos in the test set, so the Trash numbers are noisy. TrashNet photos are single objects on plain backgrounds, so cluttered phone photos will score lower. The report's 84.29% is a validation figure for a different model, so the numbers are not directly comparable.

Limitations

  1. Classes such as clothes and organic waste vary widely in appearance, which lowers accuracy.
  2. The training set is small compared with industry datasets.
  3. Collector information is entered manually, so it can go out of date.
  4. One image at a time. No multi-view or live video analysis.
  5. A single server limits speed under heavy load.

Future scope

  1. Train on a larger and more balanced dataset.
  2. Optimise the ML pipeline to cut response time.
  3. Add product condition assessment and marketplace recommendations.
  4. Add blockchain traceability for reusable goods.
  5. Fine-tune the original VGG16 with transfer learning (Colab notebook in the repo) and add multi-item detection with YOLO.
  6. Move the shared marketplace to a real backend with accounts, payments and verified collectors.

How the carbon figures are calculated

CO₂e avoided = weight × factor, where the factor is the emissions avoided by recycling a kilogram instead of landfilling it. Factors are derived by dividing the reported emission reductions by the tons recycled in the Nebraska DWEE 2025 analysis, which uses the US EPA Waste Reduction Model (WARM v16), and converting short tons to kilograms. They are US-based and indicative, so Indian values will differ. Driving equivalent uses about 0.25 kg CO₂e per km (US EPA average passenger car, about 400 g per mile). Points are 10 per kg CO₂e avoided plus 5 per entry.

Reuse ideas

Describe an item in plain words. A text-matching engine ranks reuse ideas for it.

Prototype. Built for this demo from the report's NLP reuse recommendation feature. It uses classical text processing (tokenising, stop-word removal, lemmatising, TF-IDF) plus pretrained word vectors (spaCy en_core_web_md, GloVe-based, cut to 64 dimensions) so that "old denim trousers" can find the jeans idea without sharing a word. On 36 paraphrased test queries I wrote, the blend ranked the right idea first 27 times against 26 for keywords alone (top 3: 33 against 30). That gain is small and within noise, because the library is small and keyword-rich; it would grow with a bigger library. It is not a trained language model, and it is not in the GitHub code.

Product lifespan estimator

Estimate how much useful life an item has left, and whether to keep, repair or recycle it.

Prototype. Built for this demo from the report's lifespan prediction feature. It is a transparent formula over typical lifespans, not a trained regression model, and it is not in the GitHub code.

How it is estimated

Effective life = typical life × usage factor × care factor (light 1.15, normal 1.00, heavy 0.75; careful 1.10, average 1.00, rough 0.85). Remaining life = (effective life − age) × condition factor (working 1.0, minor issues 0.7, major fault 0.3), shown with a ±25% band. Typical lifespans are planning assumptions from common industry ranges, so adjust your judgement for the specific model.

Carbon credits: can your recycling earn them?

What a credit is, who can issue one, which route fits you, what it could be worth, and how a deal is structured. Built from India's current rules and the main international standard for plastic.

Honest starting point. One carbon credit equals one tonne of CO₂e. A household that recycles 17 kg avoids about 0.056 tonnes, so you cannot register or sell credits on your own. Credits are earned at scale by registered recyclers, project developers and obligated companies. Individuals and small collectors take part by joining an aggregator or a project. Prices, fees and rule details below marked "assumption" are editable placeholders, not market quotes.

    AI Copilot runs in your browser
    Ask in plain language: “sell 25 kg copper wire”, “is my 6-year-old phone worth repairing?”, “nearest e-waste recycler to Saket”. The Copilot works out what you want, picks out the numbers, runs the right tool on this site's data and shows how it understood you.
    
  
    What this is. An on-device assistant built from keyword rules, unit and place parsing, and word-vector similarity. It is not a large language model, so it only handles the six or seven kinds of request it knows, and it says so when it is unsure. Claude is connected here: requests the Copilot does not recognise are passed to Claude, which can call the same tools.
    
  
    
    
    
    
    Hi, I am the ReNewGenie Copilot. Tell me what you want to do with your waste, in your own words. I show what I understood and which tool I ran.
    
    
    
    
    
    
    On-device answers never leave your browser. Prices are reference data from scraprates.in (6 Oct 2026) and sample collectors, not live quotes. Questions passed to Claude are sent to Claude together with the data they need, and Claude's answers can be wrong.
    
  
    
  
    Three steps, every time
    
    
    1. Understand. Score your sentence against ten kinds of request, then pull out weights (kg, quintal, tonne), materials, places, products, ages and conditions.
    2. Act. Call one of this site's own tools (collector quotes, scrap rates, the 182-place map, CO₂e factors, the forecast models, the reuse library) so the numbers come from data, not from a guess.
    3. Explain. Show the answer, then “How I understood this”: the intent, how strong the match was, what I picked out, and what I assumed.
    
    
    
  
    

    

    
  
    How the AI works
    Every AI component in ReNewGenie, what it is built from, how it was measured and where it breaks. Where I have not measured something I say so.
    
  
    
    
    Waste classifier neural network
    A convolutional network (8 conv layers, about 210,000 weights) written in plain JavaScript, plus a second model on 453 colour, edge and texture features. Their probabilities are averaged 0.6 / 0.4.
    
      
    Trained on
    TrashNet: 2,527 photos, 6 classes, split by photo
    Measured
    86.8% on 380 unseen photos (CNN alone 83.4%, features alone 81.8%); margin about ±3.5 points
    Runs
    In your browser, no upload
    Breaks when
    Photos are cluttered, dark or far from the plain backgrounds TrashNet used. Not measured on phone photos.
    
    
    Heat-map explainability
    Class activation mapping: because the last layer averages feature maps and adds one dense layer, the map of where the network looked can be computed exactly, without gradients.
    
      
    Trained on
    Nothing extra: reuses the classifier
    Measured
    Exactness checked in unit tests; usefulness is a visual aid, not a scored result
    Breaks when
    The network is right for the wrong reason: the map shows that, it does not fix it
    
    
    Confidence calibration statistics
    A table of how often the ensemble was right at each confidence level on the test photos, so “70% sure” can be read against what actually happened.
    
      
    Measured
    On the 380 test photos: at 90–100% confidence it was right 99% of the time, below 40% only 42%
    Breaks when
    Photos differ from the test set: the table then over-states trust
    
    
    Teach the AI learns on your device
    When the classifier is wrong you can say what the item really was. The page stores a 96-number fingerprint of that photo (the network's own features) and later compares new photos with your corrections, nudging the answer toward your labels.
    
      
    Technique
    k-nearest neighbours on cosine similarity of standardised CNN features, blended with the ensemble only when a close match exists
    Measured
    In simulation (no real user photos yet): about 0 points change on normal photos, and +1 to +8 points on deliberately altered photos after 3 to 10 corrections per class. Small, and not yet tested on real users.
    Stored
    Fingerprints and labels in this browser only (about 0.6 KB each, at most 200); never the photo
    Breaks when
    You label wrongly, or the new photo looks nothing like the corrected ones: then it stays out of the way
    
    
    Copilot language understanding
    Keyword rules, regular expressions for quantities and units, a place and product lexicon, and similarity of your sentence to example phrases using 64-dimension word vectors. Not a large language model.
    
      
    Measured
    On 24 new phrasings I wrote after tuning the rules: 20 of 24 intents correct with rules alone, 22 of 24 with word vectors. A tiny, developer-written test: expect lower on real users.
    Runs
    In your browser
    Breaks when
    You ask something outside its ten request types, use Hindi or heavy slang, or spell materials unusually. It then says it is unsure.
    
    
    Reuse search semantic search
    TF-IDF keyword scoring blended with word-vector similarity (spaCy en_core_web_md, reduced to 64 dimensions) over a library of reuse ideas.
    
      
    Measured
    The gain from word vectors over keywords alone is small; the Reuse ideas page shows the comparison
    Breaks when
    The library has no idea for your item: it shows a “weak match” warning
    
    
    Price forecast time-series ML
    Naive, Holt exponential smoothing and a ridge autoregression compete in rolling-origin backtests; the page reports the winner's typical error and an 80% range.
    
      
    Data
    World metal prices ending June 2017
    Breaks when
    Used for today's prices: the data is old, so it demonstrates the method only
    
    
    Route, auction and risk tools algorithms
    Exact search for pickup routes up to 8 stops, nearest-neighbour plus 2-opt beyond that, and Monte Carlo simulations for the deal and portfolio tools. These are classic algorithms, not learned models.
    
      
    Measured
    Heuristic route length checked against the exact optimum on small cases
    Breaks when
    Assumptions (road factor, speeds, probabilities) differ from your reality: they are stated on the page
    
    
    Claude features language model
    Only when this page is opened inside Claude with access allowed: a chat assistant that calls the same tools, a photo second opinion, a reuse-idea writer and a credit-project reviewer.
